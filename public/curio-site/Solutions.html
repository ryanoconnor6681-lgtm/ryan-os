Sometimes you don't need another idea. You need the thing built, and permission to hand off the building.

the lane shift

The tool can be made for you.

Stop shopping for software.

Hand AI the task.

You have an idea. It's been there for months. A small tool you keep mocking up in your head, the one that would save you twenty minutes a day if it existed. A pile of notes you keep meaning to organize. A primer you wish someone had handed you on day one.

For forty years we lived inside generic tools. Excel, Word and Photoshop were built for millions of people at once, and we bent our work to fit them. That's changing. AI now builds working software to order: one tool, for one job, for one team. The tool fits the work, where the work used to fit the tool. You ask for an outcome, and fewer things start from a blank page.

The hardest part is starting. Most people stall with a half-formed task and a feeling they should figure it out properly first. You don't need to. Describe the thing the way you'd describe it to a capable colleague: what it's for, who uses it, what good looks like. Ask for a rough version today. Then tell it what's wrong.

If handing it off feels like cheating, look at what you're handing off. There's a dial. Turned down, AI does the task and you check it. Turned up, it shows you the method, or coaches you through doing it yourself. Use the low setting for work you already know and the high one for work you want to learn. Keep the judgment either way: what's worth building, whether it's right, when it's done.

Expect it to get you most of the way. It does half, sometimes 80%, and the rest is yours. That last part is where your experience counts, so plan time for it.

If you lead a team, hold off on the big platform search. Ask each person for one task they'd hand over, build those small tools first, and keep them where everyone can reuse them. A team that owns ten small tools it understands is ahead of one waiting on a vendor.

Four small builds to start with. Each one is a task you describe and a first version you get back the same day.

I a small tool

The twenty-minute
tool
