What holds you back from AI.

Five things people say to themselves. Each one feels like a reason. Each one is a fallacy. Tap a card to flip it.

The Fallacy of Exhaustion

“I don't have time.”

turn over↻
the reframe
AI as a tool is the time…

You're expecting a tool that requires mastery. Claude doesn't. You already know how to talk. Saying "I don't have time to learn AI" is like saying "I don't have time to learn to drive — I have to keep walking everywhere." The five years of mastering Excel, CAD, Asana, Figma… [insert your program here] won't ever happen again.

The Fallacy of Guilt

“Is this cheating?”

turn over↻
the reframe
You will get left behind.

A calculator was cheating at math. Until it wasn't. Google was cheating at memory. Until it wasn't. The people running the future stopped asking this question two years ago. The real question isn't whether AI is cheating — it's whether you're still useful after the room stops calling it that.

The Fallacy of Identity

“I'm the [Excel] person.”

turn over↻
the reframe
You are more than your tool.

The Excel skill, the deck skill, the [my craft] skill — that's the ladder, not the destination. AI threatens parts of your role, not your whole job. Execution moves. Taste, judgment, and the relationships stay. That was always the real job. The scaffold is for climbing up, not for living on.

→ Inspiration turn back↺

The Fallacy of Muscle Memory

“Some day I'll be good at AI.”

turn over↻
the reframe
There is no someday.

Twenty years of software-learning trained you to think: "I'll set aside the weekend, I'll get good at this someday." AI isn't software. No curriculum. No someday. No starting line. Or the variant: "I don't know where to start" — same fallacy, different hat. You won't get good at it; you'll change what you ask of it. Stop asking how do I…. Ask what you wish. Or what you hate.

→ Mindset turn back↺

The Fallacy of Yesterday

“I tried it 8 months ago. It got it wrong.”

turn over↻
the reframe
It compounds. You don't.

It's a bias in three shapes — "I tried it months ago," "It's a fad / the new dot-com bubble," "I prompt it like a child — exhausting." All wrong the same way. Today's model isn't yesterday's. Valley of disappointment lasts ten minutes — the people who didn't quit are running circles. No genius beats it across twelve disciplines at once. Top 1% in every one, on demand. Stay short-sighted, you lose.

why curio · why now

Why this exists.

Why Curio. The problem was never information. There are ten thousand AI consultants and a tutorial for everything, and you can ask Claude itself. What's missing is someone who has actually done the work across real ground — a novel, a brand system, a forty-person rollout — and can translate it into yours. Curio ships the thinking, not the features.

Why now. Not because you're behind. Because the noise is loud. Most of what reaches you about AI isn't the change, it's commentary on the change, and sorting it is exhausting. The wave isn't going anywhere. The move isn't to hurry. It's to get quiet and ask a better question.
